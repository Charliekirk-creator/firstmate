Captain, a **three-owner team would materially help**, provided both units are ready immediately and do not contend for shared mutable resources.

**Accepted outcome and bounds:** Deliver two independent, separately checked units, then converge them into one validated result. Read-only assessment only.

| Unit | Bounded output | Owner | Prerequisites | Acceptance check |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unit A | First independent deliverable | Owner A | Scope, access, input contract | Independent executable or observable check |
| Unit B | Second independent deliverable | Owner B | Scope, access, input contract | Independent executable or observable check |
| Convergence | Combined final result | Integration owner | Accepted outputs from A and B | End-to-end integration check |

- **Direct alternative:** One owner completes A, B, convergence, and final validation serially.
- **True dependencies:** A and B have no dependency on each other. Both feed convergence.
- **Contention controls:** Each unit needs separate ownership. Any shared mutable artifact must have one designated writer.
- **Validation boundary:** Each unit may be checked independently, but one owner must control final combined validation.
- **Duplicate-work risk:** Low if output contracts and acceptance checks are distinct before work begins.
- **Integration owner:** One named owner should define input contracts up front, reconcile outputs, and own the final check.
- **Critical path:** `max(A, B) + convergence + final validation`, versus `A + B + convergence + final validation`.
- **Costs:** Additional startup, coordination, and reconciliation effort. These are justified when A and B are substantial enough that their parallel execution saves more time than convergence consumes.
- **Uncertainty:** The recommendation changes if the units modify the same semantics, rely on one scarce resource, or cannot be accepted independently.
- **Recommendation:** Use two execution owners plus one convergence owner. This is a credible team benefit because the two checked units are genuinely independent, while centralized convergence prevents fragmented accountability.
